# CPI BANGLADESH MEDICAL WAREHOUSE POLICY

**Document owner:** CPI Bangladesh Mission / Global Supply Chain and Health Functions  
**Document type:** Controlled policy framework  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Proposed for approval  
**Effective date:** To be approved  
**Review cycle:** At least annually and after a material regulatory, programmatic, product, system, or network change  
**Applies to:** CPI Bangladesh and any CPI operating unit that adopts this policy  
**Approving authority:** CPI senior management or delegated governance body  

> **Policy intent.** This policy establishes one controlled framework for safe, traceable, compliant, and accountable management of medical products across CPI programs, warehouses, field sites, emergency operations, and approved third parties. It is designed for Bangladesh implementation and can be extended to other countries through controlled local annexes.

---

## 1. Executive Policy Statement

CPI shall manage medical products as quality-critical resources. Every product shall be appropriately selected, legally authorized where required, procured or accepted from a suitable source, received against documented criteria, stored under its specified conditions, traceably issued, monitored through use or final disposition, and protected from loss, diversion, damage, falsification, and unauthorized use.

CPI shall operate a risk-based quality system across the full medical-product supply chain. The system shall cover needs assessment, standardization, procurement, donations, receipt, quarantine, quality release, storage, cold chain, inventory control, allocation, picking, dispatch, transport, delivery, returns, complaints, recalls, disposal, records, data, training, emergency response, and continual improvement. The policy is supported by approved standard operating procedures (SOPs), forms, registers, system controls, country annexes, and product-specific technical instructions.

CPI shall use the strictest applicable requirement when requirements differ. The order of precedence is: applicable law or competent-authority instruction; product label, manufacturer specification, or approved stability evidence; donor or contract requirement; CPI policy; approved SOP; and local work instruction. A less stringent internal practice shall never override a stricter legal, regulatory, safety, or product-quality requirement.

---

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to establish the governance, minimum controls, responsibilities, and performance requirements for CPI medical warehouses and related inventory locations. It seeks to ensure continuous availability of appropriate medical products while protecting quality, patient safety, program effectiveness, donor resources, staff, communities, and CPI’s reputation.

The policy converts the existing draft’s practical controls—stock cards, FEFO, physical inventory, short-expiry review, AMC, quarantine, temperature records, donation controls, and emergency stock management—into an integrated, auditable, multi-program and multi-location operating framework.

## 3. Scope and Applicability

This policy applies to all CPI personnel, volunteers, temporary workers, consultants, contractors, and partners who select, purchase, receive, store, transport, issue, use, monitor, donate, return, recall, destroy, record, or administer medical products on behalf of CPI.

It applies to the following locations and operating modes:

| Operating level | Typical CPI location | Minimum policy expectation |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | CPI organization or global function | Common policy, master-data principles, assurance, reporting, and escalation |
| L2 | Country office or mission | Regulatory compliance, country annex, network oversight, licenses, and management review |
| L3 | Project office or central warehouse | Receiving, quarantine, storage, QA release, inventory, allocation, dispatch, and reporting |
| L4 | Program site, health post, outreach unit, or sub-warehouse | Secure storage, stock records, FEFO, temperature control where applicable, issue records, and reporting |
| Temporary | Emergency store, mobile team, rented facility, transit holding area, or prepositioned stock point | Risk assessment, authorization, minimum controls, time limit, and documented closure or transition |
| Outsourced | Third-party warehouse, transporter, laboratory, disposal provider, or logistics agent | Due diligence, written agreement, defined controls, audit rights, incident reporting, and oversight |

The scope includes medicines; vaccines and other biologicals; diagnostics and in-vitro diagnostic materials; laboratory reagents; medical devices; sterile and non-sterile consumables; surgical materials; medical kits; specialized nutrition where managed through the medical supply chain; controlled medicines; hazardous or flammable materials; and related cold-chain equipment and records.

Non-medical items may use the same warehouse platform, classification, location, and internal-control framework where appropriate. The quality and regulatory controls in this policy shall be applied to non-medical products proportionately through documented risk assessment.

## 4. Policy Principles

CPI shall apply the following principles in every program and location:

1. **Patient and community safety.** Availability shall never be achieved by releasing a product of uncertain quality, expired product, falsified product, or product outside authorized use.
2. **Needs-based standardization.** Medical products shall be selected from an approved standard list unless an exception is documented and approved by the competent technical authority.
3. **Traceability.** CPI shall be able to trace a product from source to final recipient, use, return, recall, or destruction.
4. **FEFO and prevention of loss.** Dated stock shall be issued using first-expiry, first-out (FEFO) unless a documented product rule requires another method.
5. **Segregation of duties.** No person shall control an entire high-risk transaction from request through approval, receipt, adjustment, and disposal.
6. **Risk-based proportionality.** Controls shall reflect product risk, value, temperature sensitivity, misuse potential, legal status, volume, location, and emergency conditions.
7. **Data integrity.** Records shall be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original or verified, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, and available.
8. **Accountability and transparency.** CPI shall maintain evidence of decisions, communicate material risks, and provide accessible feedback and complaints channels.
9. **Do no harm through donations.** CPI shall not accept, store, transport, or donate products that are unsuitable, unauthorized, unsafe, or unlikely to be used before expiry.
10. **Continuous improvement.** Deviations, complaints, stock discrepancies, recalls, expiry losses, and audit findings shall generate learning and corrective action.

## 6. Governance and Accountability

### 6.1 Three lines of control

CPI shall operate three complementary lines of control:

| Line | Primary responsibility | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| First line: operations | Execute approved processes and identify problems | Warehouse, program, logistics, procurement, and field staff |
| Second line: technical and compliance oversight | Set standards, review risk, approve release or exceptions, and monitor compliance | Medical/health, quality, regulatory, safeguarding, finance, information security |
| Third line: independent assurance | Test whether controls work and whether management responds | Internal audit, independent review, donor audit, external inspection |

### 6.2 Required governance roles

CPI shall appoint named role holders and deputies for each operating level. Job descriptions shall define authority, qualifications, segregation of duties, escalation rights, and required training.

| Role | Minimum accountability |
|---|---|
| Senior management or governance committee | Approves policy, resources, risk appetite, significant exceptions, and management-review decisions. |
| Country Director or delegated country authority | Ensures Bangladesh implementation, legal compliance, resources, and escalation to global governance. |
| Medical or Health Lead | Approves the MSL, clinical appropriateness, medical exceptions, donation suitability, and product-quality decisions requiring clinical judgment. |
| Quality or Regulatory Lead | Owns the quality system, document control, deviations, CAPA, complaints, recalls, inspections, self-inspections, and regulatory interface. This role shall have authority independent of warehouse throughput pressure. |
| Supply Chain or Logistics Lead | Oversees procurement coordination, warehouse network, transport, capacity, continuity, and performance. |
| Central Warehouse Manager | Controls daily warehouse operations, personnel, location discipline, receiving, storage, picking, dispatch, physical security, and reporting. |
| Quality Assurance Officer or delegated qualified person | Reviews receipt exceptions, quarantine, release, temperature excursions, returns, recalls, and destruction decisions within authority. |
| Warehouse Officer | Performs receiving, put-away, stock recording, picking, packing, cycle counts, housekeeping, and exception reporting. |
| Sub-Warehouse Manager | Applies the same controls at program-site level and ensures timely reporting to the central warehouse. |
| Program Coordinator | Submits justified requests, confirms need and authorized use, receives allocations, and monitors program utilization. |
| Budget Holder | Confirms budget availability and allocation accountability. Budget approval shall not replace medical or quality approval. |
| Procurement Officer | Uses approved specifications and suppliers, maintains procurement records, and does not substitute products without technical approval. |
| Transport or Logistics Officer | Qualifies routes and transport, controls delivery evidence, and escalates delays, damage, security events, and excursions. |
| Inventory/Data Steward | Maintains item, location, UOM, lot, expiry, supplier, and reporting data quality. |
| System Administrator | Administers access and configuration without altering or deleting operational history. |
| Finance/Asset Officer | Supports valuation, donor accounting, asset custody, and reconciliation without overriding technical release controls. |

### 6.3 Segregation of duties

At minimum, the requester shall not be the sole approver; the receiver shall not be the sole releaser; the picker shall not be the sole checker for high-risk orders; the person proposing an adjustment shall not be the sole approver; and the person witnessing destruction shall not be the sole person recording it. Emergency exceptions shall be documented, time-limited, and retrospectively reviewed.

## 7. Quality Management System

CPI shall maintain a documented quality system covering the complete lifecycle of medical products. The system shall be resourced, implemented, reviewed, and improved. It shall include the following controlled elements:

- quality policy and measurable quality objectives;
- organization chart, job descriptions, delegation, and escalation;
- approved SOPs, forms, registers, and work instructions;
- training and competency assessment;
- supplier and contractor qualification;
- premises, equipment, environmental monitoring, calibration, qualification, and maintenance;
- receiving, quarantine, sampling where applicable, release, and rejection;
- inventory, FEFO, traceability, and reconciliation;
- deviations, nonconformances, complaints, recalls, returns, and CAPA;
- self-inspection, audit, management review, and regulatory inspection readiness;
- records management, data integrity, backup, access, and retention;
- emergency preparedness, continuity, and change control.

### 7.1 Document control

All controlled documents shall have an identifier, title, owner, version, approval date, effective date, review date, and approval record. Superseded copies shall be removed from points of use or clearly marked obsolete. Uncontrolled working copies shall be identified. Electronic documents shall use access permissions and version history; paper documents shall be protected from loss, damage, and unauthorized alteration.

### 7.2 Deviations and nonconformances

CPI shall record and assess every material departure from an approved process or requirement, including damaged or suspect goods, temperature excursions, missing records, stock discrepancies, unauthorized access, falsification concerns, missed monitoring, incorrect picking, late reporting, and qualification or validation failures.

Each event shall include containment, product and patient-risk assessment, investigation, root cause, disposition, CAPA, responsible owner, due date, effectiveness check, and escalation where required. A deviation shall not be closed solely because no immediate harm was observed.

### 7.3 CAPA and management review

CPI shall maintain one CAPA register for findings from deviations, complaints, recalls, audits, inspections, stock discrepancies, environmental excursions, supplier problems, and trend analysis. Management review shall examine overdue and recurring CAPAs, complaints, recalls, supplier performance, expiry loss, inventory accuracy, temperature compliance, training, resource adequacy, and emerging risks.

### 7.4 Training and competency

Before independent work, personnel shall be trained and assessed on applicable SOPs, product identification, FEFO, hygiene, security, data integrity, cold chain, controlled products, hazardous materials, emergency procedures, falsified-product detection, recall, complaints, and incident reporting. Refresher training shall be risk-based and documented. Temporary personnel and contractors shall meet the same competency expectations for their tasks.

## 8. Regulatory and Legal Compliance in Bangladesh

CPI Bangladesh shall maintain a current regulatory matrix and legal register. The matrix shall identify the product, activity, location, applicable authority, license or approval, responsible owner, issue date, expiry date, renewal lead time, conditions, evidence location, and escalation contact.

The Drugs and Cosmetics Act 2023 regulates the manufacture, import, export, purchase, sale, stocking, storage, display, and distribution of medicines and related products. Where CPI activities fall within the Act’s regulated scope, CPI shall maintain the applicable DGDA license, operate within approved premises and conditions, handle registered and appropriately authorized products, and remain inspection-ready. The Act requires WHO good practices for relevant manufacture, quality control, distribution, supply, and storage activities. [1] [2]

Controlled medicines, psychotropic substances, precursor chemicals, and relevant alcohol products shall be managed under the Narcotics Control Act 2018 and current Department of Narcotics Control requirements. CPI shall not receive, store, transport, issue, use, import, export, or dispose of controlled substances without the required authorization, license, permit, pass, record, and trained personnel. Substance classification shall be checked against current schedules and implementing requirements before procurement. [3] [4]

For imports and donations, CPI shall verify shipment-specific requirements with DGDA, Customs/NBR, CCI&E, DNC, and other relevant authorities. A donor’s approval or export-country authorization shall not be assumed to replace Bangladesh registration, import clearance, marketing authorization, controlled-substance authorization, or customs requirements. [5] [6]

CPI shall maintain an inspection-readiness file for each regulated warehouse. It shall include licenses, premises approvals, product registers, GRNs, stock records, batch and expiry records, quarantine and release records, temperature and calibration records, supplier files, complaints, recalls, disposal evidence, training records, SOPs, and previous inspection findings.

> **Legal limitation.** This policy summarizes researched regulatory interfaces, but it is not a substitute for current legal advice, certified translation, DGDA or DNC confirmation, or product-specific review. The Country Director shall obtain local compliance review before approval and whenever controlled substances, imports, donations, disposal, or new product categories are introduced.

## 9. Medical Standardization and Master Item Catalogue

### 9.1 Medical Standard List

CPI shall maintain an approved MSL for each country, program, service level, or emergency scenario. The MSL shall be based on national policy, applicable treatment protocols, epidemiology, program design, service capacity, product authorization, procurement feasibility, storage capability, and donor requirements.

The existing CPI classification shall be retained as a controlled reference and mapped into the system. It includes drugs, medical equipment, kits, nutrition, renewable medical supplies, and relevant logistics groups. The classification shall support—not replace—technical product specifications and regulatory status.

Non-standard or restricted products shall require documented justification. The existing justification codes may be used as follows:

| Code | CPI interpretation |
|---|---|
| P | Program-specific or restricted to a defined service or intervention |
| M | Medical or misuse risk requiring qualified personnel or special control |
| E | High-cost item requiring cost-effectiveness or alternative review |
| F | Follow-up item requiring additional monitoring or evaluation |
| A | Context-specific item requiring technical acknowledgement or adaptation |
| S | Second-choice item used when the preferred option is unavailable or unsuitable |

No product shall be added to the MSL solely because it was donated or is available at no cost.

### 9.2 Master item catalogue

CPI shall maintain one enterprise item master. Program, donor, budget, country, warehouse, and project shall be represented as governed dimensions rather than duplicate product codes.

Required fields shall include, as applicable:

| Data domain | Required information |
|---|---|
| Identity | Stable CPI product code/SKU, English name, local name, generic/INN name, brand, manufacturer, and approved URL/reference |
| Specification | Dosage form, strength, concentration, size, composition, model, dimensions, pack size, and technical specification |
| Classification | Group, family, sub-family, therapeutic class, medical/non-medical nature, program eligibility, and justification code |
| Regulatory | Registration/authorization status, license or approval reference, controlled-product status, and restrictions |
| Inventory | Default UOM, conversion rules, batch/lot requirement, serial requirement, expiry/retest requirement, and package hierarchy |
| Storage | Labelled temperature, humidity, light, hazardous, segregation, and handling requirements |
| Supply | Approved suppliers, lead time, minimum shelf-life requirement, source/donor restrictions, and approved alternatives |
| Planning | Reorder point, min/max, safety stock, reorder quantity, forecast method, AMC window, and review frequency |
| Finance and reporting | Finance account code, HS code where applicable, unit cost, currency, donor/program mapping, and CPI standard level |
| Lifecycle | Active, restricted, suspended, discontinued, obsolete, or replaced status with effective dates |

Mandatory fields shall be validated before activation. Duplicate detection, controlled synonyms, UOM conversion, expiry formats, and product status shall be governed by an approved change process.

## 10. Warehouse Network and Facility Design

CPI shall maintain a documented warehouse hierarchy and location register. Each warehouse, zone, rack, shelf, bin, quarantine area, cold-chain unit, in-transit location, and disposal location shall have a unique identifier.

### 10.1 Minimum zones

Each central warehouse shall provide, as applicable, clearly identified and access-controlled areas for:

- receiving and inspection;
- quarantine and pending release;
- approved usable stock;
- controlled medicines and high-risk products;
- cold-chain products;
- hazardous, flammable, or incompatible materials;
- returns, recalls, rejected, damaged, and expired stock;
- picking, packing, and dispatch;
- documentation and equipment storage; and
- waste or disposal staging.

Physical separation is preferred. Where space or emergency conditions make physical separation impossible, CPI shall use equivalent secure segregation with status labels, system blocks, access restriction, and documented risk acceptance.

### 10.2 Premises and security

Premises shall be clean, dry, adequately ventilated and lit, protected from flooding, water ingress, pests, direct sunlight, unauthorized access, theft, tampering, fire, and unsafe electrical conditions. Products shall be stored off the floor and positioned to permit inspection, cleaning, ventilation, and safe handling.

CPI shall maintain pest control, sanitation, waste, fire protection, preventive maintenance, generator/UPS, access control, visitor, key, and incident procedures. Eating, drinking, smoking, and personal storage are prohibited in product areas.

### 10.3 Capacity and site approval

Before opening or materially expanding a warehouse, CPI shall assess capacity for normal, peak, emergency, quarantine, returned, and disposal stock. The assessment shall consider product compatibility, pallet and shelving design, cold-chain capacity, power reliability, loading access, security, evacuation, occupational safety, flood and cyclone risk, connectivity, and alternate storage.

## 11. Procurement, Supplier Qualification, and Donations

### 11.1 Procurement

Procurement shall use approved specifications, the MSL, approved suppliers, documented approvals, and applicable CPI procurement controls. Substitution of active ingredient, strength, dosage form, pack size, manufacturer, or storage condition requires technical approval before order or receipt.

### 11.2 Supplier qualification

CPI shall maintain a risk-based supplier file containing authorization/licensing evidence, product quality information, storage and distribution capability, performance history, complaints, recalls, deviations, subcontracting, conflicts of interest, and reassessment status. Higher-risk suppliers shall receive enhanced due diligence or audit. Supplier approval, suspension, reapproval, and performance review shall be documented.

### 11.3 Donations

Before accepting a donation, the receiving program and technical authority shall confirm:

- a documented need and approved recipient;
- product identity, registration or authorization, quality, labeling, and remaining shelf life;
- suitability for the intended users, service level, language, and treatment protocols;
- storage, transport, customs, regulatory, and disposal requirements;
- batch/lot, expiry, manufacturer, and quantity information;
- the donor’s responsibility for transport, documentation, and costs; and
- a plan for unused, expired, unsuitable, recalled, or damaged products.

Unsolicited, short-dated, unlabelled, damaged, clinically inappropriate, or unauthorized donations shall be rejected or quarantined pending an authorized decision. Every accepted donation shall have a donation certificate and registry entry. The recipient shall sign confirmation of receipt. Controlled substances require separate authority approval and documentation. CPI shall never donate products that it would not ethically or safely accept itself. [7]

## 12. Receiving and Quality Release

All incoming medical products shall be received against a purchase order, transfer order, donation record, return authorization, or other approved reference.

### 12.1 Receiving checks

The receiver and, for higher-risk products, an independent checker shall verify:

- supplier, donor, or sending location;
- order, waybill, packing list, and package count;
- product code, description, strength, dosage form, specification, and UOM;
- quantity and unit conversion;
- batch/lot and serial numbers where applicable;
- expiry or retest date and remaining shelf life;
- label, seal, packaging, contamination, tampering, leakage, and damage;
- temperature data, VVM, freeze indicator, or other shipping evidence where applicable;
- required certificates, authorization, and product information; and
- status decision: accepted, partially accepted, quarantined, rejected, or pending information.

A GRN shall record discrepancies and attach photographs, temperature records, carrier documents, and supplier communication where relevant.

### 12.2 Quarantine and release

Products shall remain in quarantine until acceptance criteria are met. Quarantine is mandatory for products that are damaged, incomplete, short-dated beyond approved limits, temperature-exposed, suspected falsified, unlabelled, unauthorized, returned, recalled, or otherwise nonconforming.

Only an authorized quality or technical role may release stock to usable status. The WMS or stock card shall prevent issue of quarantine and rejected stock. Release decisions shall be traceable to the product, batch/lot, quantity, evidence reviewed, decision-maker, date, and conditions.

## 13. Storage and Environmental Control

Products shall be stored according to the product label, manufacturer instructions, approved technical information, and applicable law. Generic warehouse temperature ranges shall not override product-specific conditions.

The draft’s ambient reference ranges may be used only when consistent with product information and approved facility qualification. The following categories are examples, not universal rules:

| Category | Example range | Control requirement |
|---|---:|---|
| Freezer | Product-specific, often below 0°C | Use only for products labelled for freezing; prevent inappropriate freezing of freeze-sensitive products |
| Refrigerator | Commonly +2°C to +8°C | Applies only where the product label requires it |
| Cool | Commonly +8°C to +15°C | Use only when product information permits |
| Controlled ambient | Commonly +15°C to +25°C | Use only when label and qualified mapping support it |
| Other | Manufacturer-defined | Record the exact requirement in the item master and temperature matrix |

### 13.1 Qualification and mapping

New or materially changed temperature-controlled rooms, refrigerators, freezers, vehicles, containers, and monitoring systems shall be qualified before use. Mapping shall identify hot and cold spots under empty and normally loaded conditions, consider seasonal variation where relevant, and be repeated after significant change or unexplained variability. The interval shall be risk-based and documented; a three-year example shall not be treated as a universal legal requirement. [8]

### 13.2 Monitoring and calibration

CPI shall use monitoring equipment suitable for the product and risk. Temperature-sensitive storage shall use recorded monitoring, preferably continuous where feasible, with sensors positioned using mapping results. Humidity shall be monitored where product requirements or risk require it. Equipment shall be calibrated against a traceable reference at a defined interval, normally at least annually unless a justified alternative is approved. Calibration certificates and out-of-tolerance assessments shall be retained. [8] [9]

### 13.3 Alarm and excursion response

Alarm limits shall be based on product requirements and qualified operating ranges. Every alarm, out-of-range event, monitoring gap, power failure, refrigeration failure, or suspected freeze shall trigger:

1. immediate protection and identification of affected stock;
2. quarantine marked “DO NOT USE”;
3. preservation of original monitoring and device data;
4. notification to the warehouse manager and quality/technical lead;
5. assessment of product, batch, quantity, duration, maximum/minimum exposure, VVM or indicator status, and equipment history;
6. technical or manufacturer advice where stability is not established;
7. a documented release, restricted-use, return, destruction, or other disposition decision; and
8. CAPA and effectiveness review where the event indicates a system weakness.

A VVM detects cumulative heat exposure only. It does not detect freezing and does not replace temperature records or freeze indicators. CTC handling shall be used only when specifically authorized by the product label and program instructions. [9]

### 13.4 Maintenance and continuity

CPI shall maintain preventive maintenance for premises, HVAC, refrigerators, freezers, generators, UPS, alarms, sensors, shelving, pallets, forklifts, fire equipment, and security systems. Each location shall maintain a power-failure and equipment-failure plan with alternate storage, transfer equipment, fuel or backup power, responsible contacts, escalation triggers, emergency transport, and documentation.

## 14. Inventory Control and Stock Records

### 14.1 Stock card and electronic record

One stock card or electronic stock record shall represent one controlled item definition, including INN, strength, dosage form, specification, UOM, product code, and required batch/serial dimensions. Each movement shall be recorded as a separate transaction. Records shall identify date, source or destination, quantity, batch/lot, expiry, reference document, operator, and resulting balance.

Paper stock cards shall remain with the product or in an authorized warehouse filing system. They shall not be kept only in an office away from the stock. When a card is full, the closing balance shall be transferred to a new card, the old card archived, and the transfer independently checked.

### 14.2 Stock movements

Every movement shall use a controlled transaction type:

- receipt from procurement, donation, transfer, or return;
- put-away or location change;
- issue to program, health unit, partner, or authorized recipient;
- transfer between warehouses;
- allocation or commitment;
- adjustment after approved investigation;
- return to warehouse or supplier;
- quarantine, release, recall, or reclassification;
- damage, expiry, loss, or disposal; and
- in-transit movement.

No negative balance, backdated movement, undocumented emergency withdrawal, or deletion of posted history is permitted. Corrections shall use controlled reversal or adjustment transactions linked to the original record.

### 14.3 Controlled products

Controlled medicines shall be stored in a locked, access-controlled location. The controlled-drug register shall record product, strength, form, batch, expiry, receipt, issue, destination, authorized recipient, quantity, balance, signatures, and supporting documents. Reconciliation shall occur at every receipt and issue and at a defined periodic frequency. Any discrepancy shall be immediately secured, investigated, reported to the responsible authority, and escalated under applicable law.

### 14.4 FEFO and short expiry

The WMS and physical layout shall support FEFO. Items with less than six months’ remaining shelf life shall be listed and reviewed against expected consumption, transfer options, donor conditions, and regulatory restrictions. Any issue of short-dated stock requires confirmation that the receiving location can use it safely before expiry.

Products shall not be issued after expiry. A product expiring by month shall be treated according to its label, applicable law, and approved technical instruction; CPI shall not create a universal rule where product labeling or regulation differs.

## 15. Forecasting, Replenishment, and Availability

CPI shall plan inventory by item, location, program, service level, and scenario. Forecasting shall consider consumption, stockouts, seasonality, planned scale-up or closure, morbidity or service targets, pipeline stock, lead time, supplier reliability, emergency demand, storage capacity, shelf life, and funding.

### 15.1 AMC

AMC shall be calculated from a defined consumption dataset and period. Consumption shall be distinguished from all outgoing stock. Donations, transfers, loans, returns, expiry removals, and non-program distributions shall not be treated as routine consumption. Stockout months shall be flagged and adjusted where they suppressed observed consumption. The calculation method, period, exclusions, and approver shall be documented. [10]

### 15.2 Reorder and buffer parameters

Each active item-location combination shall have approved planning parameters where applicable:

- review period;
- supplier lead time and variability;
- reorder point;
- minimum and maximum stock;
- running stock;
- safety or buffer stock;
- reorder quantity;
- emergency threshold;
- maximum stock constrained by shelf life and capacity; and
- responsible owner and review date.

The draft formula remains useful as a planning concept:

> **Warning or reorder level = consumption during lead time + security stock.**

For example, if lead time is two months and security stock is one month, a product with AMC of 2,000 units has a planning warning level of 6,000 units. This is an illustrative calculation, not a universal CPI parameter.

### 15.3 Availability escalation

The warehouse or WMS shall alert the program and supply-chain leads when stock approaches an approved warning level, is projected to stock out, is overstocked, or is at risk of expiry. The response may include redistribution, supplier acceleration, approved substitution, borrowing, emergency procurement, program adjustment, or temporary allocation control. Decisions shall be recorded with owner and due date.

## 16. Picking, Allocation, Dispatch, and Transport

Stock shall be issued only against an approved requisition, allocation, emergency request, transfer request, donation certificate, or other authorized instruction.

### 16.1 Picking and checking

The picker shall select product, strength/specification, batch/lot, expiry, serial, quantity, and UOM against the approved request using FEFO. An independent checker shall verify high-risk, controlled, cold-chain, expensive, or clinically sensitive orders. The check shall be recorded.

### 16.2 Packing and dispatch

Packing shall protect products from damage, contamination, leakage, theft, tampering, light, humidity, and temperature exposure. Cartons shall be numbered against the packing list. Dispatch documentation shall identify sender, recipient, product, quantity, batch/lot, expiry, storage requirement, transport conditions, documents, and delivery evidence.

Medical kits shall retain contents, product identity, instructions, packing lists, and lot/expiry traceability. Opening or modifying a kit requires technical authorization and an updated kit record.

### 16.3 Transport

CPI shall use suitable, clean, secure, and identifiable vehicles and containers. Temperature-sensitive routes, vehicles, passive containers, and packing configurations shall be qualified where risk requires. Transport contractors shall be approved and trained. Delays, damage, tampering, theft, temperature excursions, and failed delivery shall be reported immediately and handled through deviation and incident procedures. [11]

### 16.4 Delivery confirmation

The receiving unit shall confirm item, quantity, condition, batch/lot, expiry, and temperature status where applicable. Unfilled quantities, substitutions, discrepancies, and follow-up commitments shall be recorded. The WMS shall not mark a shipment complete merely because it left the warehouse.

## 17. Returns, Complaints, Recalls, and Falsified Products

### 17.1 Returns

All returned medical products shall enter quarantine. The return record shall state the product, batch/lot, expiry, quantity, reason, storage and transport history, condition, and receiving date. Reissue is permitted only after an authorized assessment confirms product quality, traceability, packaging integrity, and acceptable storage history.

### 17.2 Complaints

CPI shall maintain a complaint register and triage complaints by severity. Complaints involving suspected harm, falsification, contamination, wrong product, controlled-product diversion, or widespread quality risk shall be escalated immediately to the Quality/Regulatory Lead, Medical Lead, Country Director, manufacturer, and competent authority where required.

### 17.3 Recall

CPI shall maintain a rapid, tested recall procedure. The system shall identify affected batches/serials, every recipient and location, quantities issued, quantities recovered, outstanding quantities, communications, transport, storage, disposition, and closure evidence. Recalled stock shall be blocked, segregated, secured, and reconciled. A mock recall shall be conducted at least annually or at a documented risk-based interval approved by quality governance. [12]

### 17.4 Suspected falsified products

Suspect falsified products shall not be used, returned to ordinary stock, donated, or destroyed before evidence is preserved and the responsible authority is consulted, unless immediate safety action is required. CPI shall secure the product, record source and distribution history, notify relevant technical and regulatory contacts, and investigate supplier and network exposure.

---

## 18. Expired, Damaged, Rejected, and Hazardous Products

Expired, damaged, rejected, recalled, suspect, or otherwise unusable products shall be removed from usable stock immediately and moved to a clearly marked quarantine or disposal area. The stock record and expired/damaged register shall record item, batch/serial, expiry, quantity, reason, location, date, responsible person, authorization, and final disposition.

CPI shall use an approved, safe, environmentally responsible disposal route consistent with applicable law, product characteristics, donor conditions, worker protection, and competent-authority instructions. Controlled medicines, cytotoxic or hazardous products, chemicals, sharps, biological materials, and medical devices may require different methods and specialist vendors.

Destruction shall be witnessed where required and supported by a certificate or equivalent evidence. Disposal shall not be delayed until quarantine becomes a permanent dump. Related costs and financial losses shall be reported for management review and prevention planning.

---

## 19. Physical Inventory, Cycle Counts, and Reconciliation

A physical inventory shall verify physical stock against the theoretical record, identify expired or damaged stock, detect short expiry, confirm arrangement and segregation, and identify causes of discrepancy.

### 19.1 Minimum counting controls

- Counts shall be planned with a defined cut-off and transaction control.
- At least two people shall count high-risk or material-value stock.
- Products shall be counted by item, UOM, batch/lot, expiry, serial, and location as applicable.
- Expired and damaged stock shall be segregated during the count.
- Count sheets shall be signed and dated; electronic counts shall preserve operator and timestamp.
- Variances shall be investigated before adjustment.
- Material adjustments require independent approval and documented root cause.
- Results shall be reviewed by program, warehouse, item class, and risk category.

### 19.2 Frequency

CPI shall use risk-based cycle counting. Controlled, high-value, fast-moving, temperature-sensitive, lot/serial-tracked, near-expiry, and historically inaccurate items shall be counted more frequently. Each warehouse shall conduct a full physical inventory at least annually and more often where risk, donor, regulatory, or management requirements dictate. The existing draft’s quarterly review for intermediary or field pharmacies shall be retained as a minimum starting point unless a documented risk assessment approves another frequency.

### 19.3 Variance investigation

Investigations shall consider counting error, UOM conversion, similar-item confusion, unposted receipt or issue, return, undocumented emergency withdrawal, damage, expiry, theft, system integration error, unauthorized adjustment, and location misplacement. Repeated same-direction variance shall be treated as a potential control or safeguarding concern, not merely a clerical error.

---

## 20. Emergency Operations and Continuity

CPI shall maintain an emergency medical supply plan for each country and high-risk location. The plan shall cover outbreak, displacement, cyclone, flood, fire, security incident, access disruption, sudden program scale-up, power loss, cold-chain failure, supplier interruption, border delay, system outage, warehouse loss, and staff shortage.

The plan shall define:

- scenario-based demand, service level, and stock assumptions;
- prepositioned stock and rotation plan;
- alternate suppliers, warehouses, routes, transporters, and power sources;
- emergency request and approval workflow;
- authority for emergency release, substitution, borrowing, or donation;
- paper fallback records and later system reconciliation;
- minimum staffing and safety controls;
- cold-chain contingency and relocation;
- communications and escalation contacts;
- security and access controls; and
- return to normal operations and after-action review.

Emergency status shall not waive product quality, traceability, controlled-substance, regulatory, or safety controls. Where a control cannot be completed before immediate life-saving release, the exception shall be documented, approved by the highest available authority, and retrospectively reviewed as soon as practicable.

CPI may use recognized emergency kits such as the WHO/UNICEF Interagency Emergency Health Kit as a planning reference, but kit contents shall be adapted to local treatment protocols, service level, population, authorization, storage capacity, and trained personnel. [13]

---

## 21. Multi-Program, Multi-Location, and Global Scalability

CPI shall maintain one common control model with configurable local implementation. The WMS or controlled records shall support:

- organization, country, project, program, service site, warehouse, zone, bin, and in-transit hierarchy;
- one product master with program, donor, funding, and budget dimensions;
- batch, lot, expiry, retest, and serial tracking according to risk;
- central and sub-warehouse balances;
- stock ownership, allocation, commitment, and available status;
- transfers, returns, loans, donations, recalls, and disposal;
- offline or paper fallback and controlled synchronization;
- barcode or other machine-readable capture where feasible;
- role-based access by country, program, warehouse, and data type;
- multiple languages, currencies, time zones, UOMs, and calendars;
- country-specific regulatory and retention configuration; and
- reporting by item, program, donor, warehouse, location, lot, expiry, value, and status.

A new country or program shall not be added to the system without a documented readiness assessment covering legal requirements, product authorization, supplier qualification, warehouse capacity, temperature control, disposal, data protection, donor conditions, roles, training, connectivity, and reporting.

---

## 22. Data Governance and System Controls

### 22.1 Access

The system shall use unique user identities, least privilege, strong authentication, site/program scope, prompt deactivation of leavers, periodic access recertification, and time-bound elevated access. Shared accounts are prohibited except where a formally approved technical exception is unavoidable.

### 22.2 Audit trail

The system shall preserve a non-overwriting audit trail for master-data changes, stock transactions, approvals, adjustments, overrides, access changes, exports, integrations, and configuration. The audit trail shall identify user, date/time, old value, new value, reason, approver, and source where available.

### 22.3 Backup and continuity

CPI shall maintain backup, restoration, disaster recovery, outage, offline synchronization, and cybersecurity procedures. Restoration tests shall be documented. During outage, paper records shall use controlled forms and be reconciled into the system after restoration without duplicate or lost transactions.

### 22.4 Data quality

CPI shall measure completeness, accuracy, validity, uniqueness, consistency, and timeliness of item, location, supplier, lot, expiry, stock, and transaction data. WMS, procurement, finance, transport, and program systems shall have defined system-of-record ownership and reconciliation procedures.

---

## 23. Reporting and Performance Management

Each warehouse shall provide a monthly stock statement and each program shall provide a consumption report. Reports shall distinguish receipts, consumption, transfers, donations, loans, returns, expiry, damage, adjustments, and physical stock.

### 23.1 Minimum KPI dashboard

| KPI | Definition and reporting dimension |
|---|---|
| Stockout rate and days | Products or service locations below approved availability threshold, by program and location |
| Order-line fill rate | Complete lines delivered against approved request, including approved substitutions separately |
| Inventory record accuracy | Accurate count lines and quantity/value against lines counted, by risk class |
| FEFO compliance | Lines issued according to approved FEFO rule, with justified overrides separately reported |
| Expiry and damage loss | Quantity and value removed from usable stock, by cause, product, program, supplier, and warehouse |
| Near-expiry exposure | Quantity and value within approved short-expiry window and action status |
| Receiving cycle time | Arrival to GRN and release, separated for ordinary and cold-chain products |
| Pick accuracy | Correct item, quantity, UOM, batch/lot, expiry, and serial at dispatch |
| Cold-chain compliance | Monitoring completeness, excursion count, response time, and product disposition |
| Recall traceability | Time and percentage of affected stock/recipients identified and reconciled |
| Controlled-stock variance | Count and quantity variance, investigation status, and escalation |
| CAPA performance | Open, overdue, recurring, and effectiveness-verified CAPAs |
| Supplier performance | On-time delivery, quality rejection, short-dated supply, discrepancy, and complaint rates |
| Data quality | Completeness, duplicate, invalid UOM, missing expiry/lot, and unresolved master-data errors |

Targets shall be set after baseline measurement and approved by the relevant governance body. A target shall always include numerator, denominator, exclusions, period, data source, owner, and escalation threshold.

### 23.2 Reporting cadence

- Daily: operational exceptions, alarms, urgent stockouts, security events, and pending releases.
- Weekly: stockout risk, expiry, emergency requests, open receiving discrepancies, and cold-chain events.
- Monthly: stock statement, consumption, AMC, reconciliation, KPI dashboard, and program review.
- Quarterly: supplier, parameter, access, master-data, and CAPA trend review.
- Annually: full inventory, self-inspection, mock recall, policy review, emergency exercise, and management review.

---

## 24. Self-Inspection, Audit, and Management Review

CPI shall conduct documented self-inspections using competent personnel sufficiently independent of the activity under review. The review shall cover premises, security, sanitation, temperature, documents, stock, quarantine, controlled products, traceability, dispatch, disposal, training, data, contractors, and CAPA.

Findings shall be risk-rated, assigned to owners, given due dates, and verified for effectiveness. Serious or critical findings shall trigger immediate containment and senior escalation. Donor, DGDA, DNC, customs, partner, or other external inspection findings shall enter the same CAPA system.

Management review shall evaluate whether the quality system remains suitable, effective, resourced, and compliant. Decisions shall include action, owner, deadline, resource need, and follow-up evidence.

## 26. Implementation and Transition Plan

CPI shall implement this policy in controlled phases.

| Phase | Priority actions | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Governance and legal readiness | Approve policy; appoint owners; complete Bangladesh legal/regulatory matrix; identify warehouses and products in scope | Signed policy, role matrix, legal register, warehouse register |
| 2. Baseline and risk assessment | Assess premises, capacity, security, cold chain, data, suppliers, controlled products, disposal, and emergency readiness | Gap assessment, risk register, corrective-action plan |
| 3. Master data and network | Consolidate MEDEX references, existing central and field inventories, assets, standard catalogue, locations, UOMs, batch/serial rules, and program dimensions | Approved item master, location master, data-quality report |
| 4. SOP and form control | Approve receiving, storage, FEFO, cold chain, dispatch, returns, recalls, disposal, inventory, emergency, and data SOPs | Controlled SOP set, forms, training matrix |
| 5. Facility and equipment controls | Zone warehouses; quarantine stock; map and qualify cold chain; calibrate devices; establish maintenance, pest, fire, and security controls | Qualification and calibration files, inspection checklist, contingency plan |
| 6. Pilot and migration | Pilot one central warehouse and selected sub-warehouses; reconcile opening balances; test transfers, batch/expiry, recall, offline, and reporting | Pilot report, signed opening inventory, test evidence |
| 7. Scale and assurance | Train all locations; deploy in waves; run mock recall and emergency exercise; complete self-inspection and management review | Training records, recall test, exercise report, audit and CAPA closure |

The implementation shall not declare a warehouse compliant merely because software is installed. Compliance requires trained people, approved processes, suitable facilities, reliable records, demonstrated controls, and management oversight.

## 27. Exceptions and Change Control

Exceptions shall be rare, documented, risk-assessed, approved by the designated authority, time-limited, and reviewed before expiry. An exception shall identify the requirement, reason, affected products and locations, risk, compensating controls, owner, approval, start date, end date, and closure evidence.

Changes to products, suppliers, warehouse layout, equipment, software, data structure, programs, service levels, storage conditions, legal requirements, or SOPs shall use change control. Changes shall be assessed for quality, regulatory, patient, donor, data, training, capacity, and continuity impact before implementation.

---

## 28. Non-Compliance and Escalation

Failure to comply with this policy may create patient, legal, financial, safeguarding, donor, or reputational risk. CPI shall respond proportionately through immediate containment, retraining, corrective action, access restriction, supplier suspension, product quarantine, regulatory notification, investigation, or disciplinary action under applicable CPI procedures.

The following require immediate escalation:

- suspected falsified, contaminated, or harmful product;
- controlled-substance discrepancy or diversion;
- serious cold-chain failure or uncertain product stability;
- significant stock loss or repeated unexplained variance;
- unauthorized distribution or donation;
- regulatory notice, seizure, inspection finding, or license risk;
- recall affecting a distributed product; and
- warehouse loss, fire, flood, security breach, or inability to protect stock.

---

## 29. Approval and Review

This policy becomes effective only after approval by the designated CPI authority and completion of the Bangladesh legal and regulatory review. The policy owner shall review it at least annually and sooner when there is a material change in law, product scope, program model, warehouse network, technology, donor requirement, or a serious incident.

The next review shall specifically verify:

- DGDA and DNC license and authorization requirements;
- current product registration and donation requirements;
- disposal and environmental controls;
- cold-chain equipment and product matrix;
- retention schedule and data-security controls;
- KPI baselines and targets;
- WMS audit-trail and offline capabilities; and
- effectiveness of recalls, CAPA, emergency exercises, and self-inspections.
